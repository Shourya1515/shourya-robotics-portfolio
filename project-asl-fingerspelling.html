Real-Time ASL Fingerspelling Recognition

  • CS 5330 Pattern Recognition & Computer Vision
  • Northeastern · Spring 2026
  • Team of 3
  • PyTorch
  • MediaPipe
  • OpenCV

A live webcam app that recognizes 24 static American Sign Language letters, and the benchmark study behind it: three CNN architectures trained on the Kaggle ASL Alphabet dataset, then tested on photos our team took ourselves — which exposed a large domain gap.

  • 24
    static letters, from 72,000 benchmark images
  • 27–30 FPS
    end-to-end live app on a laptop GPU (target: 15 FPS)
  • 100%
    ResNet18 accuracy on the in-distribution test split
  • −67 pts
    ResNet18 accuracy on 240 team-collected photos

Gallery

Data

The Kaggle ASL Alphabet dataset (87,000 images, 29 classes), filtered to the 24 static letters — J and Z need motion — for 72,000 images split 80/10/10. To test real-world performance, the team also photographed 240 images of our own hands (10 per letter) with smartphone cameras, auto-cropped with MediaPipe.

Models

  • Custom CNN: a 5-block network trained from scratch (7.5M parameters).
  • ResNet18: transfer learning with the last residual block and the head fine-tuned.
  • MobileNetV2: frozen backbone with a small trainable head (334K parameters).

Live App

OpenCV captures the webcam at 1280×720, MediaPipe Hands finds the hand, and a square crop resized to 224×224 goes to the classifier. A 7-frame smoothing window steadies the prediction, and a spelling mode builds words letter by letter. It runs at 27–30 FPS end-to-end on an RTX 5070 Ti laptop GPU.

Key Finding

Near-perfect benchmark accuracy did not transfer: every model lost 66–71 percentage points on real photos, where lighting, backgrounds, hand shapes and cameras differ from the dataset. Retraining ResNet18 on Kaggle plus team data kept 99.90% on the Kaggle test set, and that model powers the live app. The lesson: validate perception models on deployment-like data early.
