Bio-Inspired Multi-Morphing Omnidirectional Robot

  • IEEE ICRA 2024 · Late-Breaking Results poster

  • Undergraduate capstone · Jan – Apr 2024

A spider- and crab-inspired wheeled quadruped that changes its footprint to squeeze through tight spaces and drives in any direction. The design was presented as a late-breaking results poster at IEEE ICRA 2024 in Yokohama by an eight-author team from Amrita Vishwa Vidyapeetham and Sony India Software Centre. My part was the leg kinematics: forward and inverse kinematics and the Jacobian.

Design

Inspired by spiders and crabs, the robot stands on four 3-DOF legs that end in wheels. Folding and extending the legs changes its footprint (“multi-morphing”), so it can shrink or expand to fit through confined spaces, while the wheels let it move omnidirectionally.

Kinematics — My Contribution

  • Modeled each leg with Denavit–Hartenberg parameters (base, wheel, ankle, knee, hip and twist frames).
  • Derived forward kinematics and the body Jacobian JB that maps joint rates to body velocity (ẋ, ẏ, ω).
  • Inverse kinematics for the legs, with body-velocity commands mapped to joint rates through the Jacobian pseudo-inverse JB+ = JBT(JBJBT)−1.

Design Analysis

The condition number κ(JB) = λmax/λmin measures how evenly the robot can move in every direction. Sweeping it over wheel radius, wheel offset (0.03 m) and steering angle identified geometries with κ close to 1 — well-conditioned, near-isotropic motion — and the feasible steering ranges.

Control Architecture

A desired body velocity goes to a Sony Spresense main controller, which applies the Jacobian with encoder feedback and drives a secondary controller for the wheel and steering motors. Commands arrive over a Wi-Fi API in an axis.direction.RPM format (for example X.FORWARD.11). The planned sensor suite included LiDAR, an IMU, a camera and ultrasonic sensors.

Validation & Status

The kinematics were verified in simulation (CoppeliaSim). Building a full hardware prototype was left as future work.

Publication

“Design Considerations for Bioinspired Multi-morphing Omnidirectional Robot,” Late-Breaking Results poster, IEEE ICRA 2024, Yokohama, Japan.

Paramesh S.P.S., Ruvanthika T., Sanjeev S., Barathwaj A., Shourya V., Sharan R., Anil S., Rammohan S. — Department of Mechanical Engineering, Amrita Vishwa Vidyapeetham, Coimbatore, with Sony India Software Centre, Bengaluru. Supported by Sony’s Sensing Solutions University Program.
